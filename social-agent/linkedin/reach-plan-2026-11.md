# LinkedIn reach plan: 11 Nov – 30 Nov 2026

Profile: Saktheeswaran S (personal). Replaces the earlier daily plan.

## Why the old plan didn't work

- Generic tips and lists with AI posters look like ads. People scroll past.
- Posts went out by scheduler while Sakthi was offline, so nobody was there in the first hour to reply.
- Daily posting with low engagement splits the little reach there is.
- Sakthi's best posts before were personal: the Astra Nexora launch, "still learning at 21", "I run a full agency alone", a real Search Console breakdown.

## New rules

1. **3 posts a week: Mon, Wed, Fri, 9:00–10:00 AM IST.** Posted by Sakthi from the LinkedIn app (Typefully's free quota is used up until 1 Dec, and being online at posting time matters more).
2. **Personal, opinion or real story.** No generic tip lists.
3. **Text-only, a real photo of Sakthi, or a real screenshot.** No AI-generated people or stock-style posters on these.
4. **Only real details.** Anything in [brackets] must be replaced with what really happened. If it didn't happen, skip that post or that line.
5. **No links in the post. 0–3 hashtags.** Don't edit the post in the first hour.
6. **No client brand names.** "A clinic I work with" is fine. Never mention Guggulr.

## The routine that drives reach (this matters more than the post)

| When | What |
|------|------|
| 15 min before posting | Leave 5 thoughtful comments (2–3 lines each, not "Great post") on posts by founders, marketers and business owners in India |
| Post time | Post, then stay on LinkedIn for 30–60 minutes |
| First hour | Reply to every comment, and end each reply with a question so the conversation continues |
| Every day (10–15 min) | 10 more comments on others' posts + 10–20 connection requests to business owners, founders and marketers in Tamil Nadu / India |

Reach starts with your own network. A small network means a small first audience, so the connection requests are not optional.

## 2 · Fri 13 Nov: It's a follow-up problem

Format: text only.

```
Unpopular opinion:

Most small businesses don't have a marketing problem.
They have a follow-up problem.

The enquiries are already coming in.

→ A WhatsApp message at 9 PM that gets a reply the next afternoon
→ A missed call nobody calls back
→ A quote sent, and then silence
→ "I'll think about it", and nobody ever asks again

By the time the business replies, the customer has already asked someone else.

Before you spend another rupee on ads, check this:
How long does it take you to reply to a new enquiry?

Under 5 minutes: you're winning more than you think.
Over an hour: you're paying for leads and handing them to your competitors.

Fix the follow-up first. Then buy more traffic.

Business owners, be honest: what's your average reply time?
```

## 8 · Fri 27 Nov: Discounts and Black Friday

Format: text only.

```
Black Friday is here, and every brand is shouting "50% OFF".

Here's my problem with it:

Every discount teaches your customers one thing:
"Wait. It'll get cheaper."

Then they stop buying at full price. And you need a bigger discount next time.

The businesses I respect most don't compete on price. They give a reason to buy now:
→ A bonus, not a discount
→ Something extra for loyal customers
→ A real deadline

Cut your price, and you cut your brand with it.

Are you running a Black Friday offer this year, or skipping it?
```
