## Tue 10 Nov: Wix vs WordPress vs custom code

Poster text:
- WEBSITE · WHICH ONE?
- Wix, WordPress or custom code?
- **The right answer depends on one question.**
- Wix: easiest to start · monthly plan · less control, slower to grow
- WordPress: flexible, thousands of plugins · needs hosting and regular updates
- Custom code: fastest and fully yours · needs a developer for changes
- Ask first: **Who will update the site, and how often?**

```
"Should I build my website on Wix, WordPress, or get it custom coded?"

I get asked this a lot. There's no single best answer, but there is a right answer for your business.

Wix
✓ Easiest to start, drag and drop
✓ Hosting included in the monthly plan
✗ Less control, harder to grow with complex needs

WordPress
✓ Very flexible, plugins for almost everything
✓ Huge community, easy to find help
✗ Needs hosting, updates and security care

Custom code
✓ Fastest and fully yours
✓ Built exactly around your business
✗ You need a developer for most changes

The question that decides it:
Who will update the site, and how often?

Your team will edit it themselves → pick something easy, like Wix or WordPress
A few updates a month → WordPress works well
You need custom features built around your business → custom code

Which one is your website on right now?

#WebDesign #SmallBusiness #WordPress
```

## Wed 11 Nov: 3 ways to reply to Google reviews

Poster text:
- GOOGLE REVIEWS
- Don't just reply "Thank you!"
- **Every reply is read by your next customer.**
- ★★★★★ "Thank you, [Name]! So glad the [service] went well. See you next time."
- ★★★ "Thanks for the honest feedback, [Name]. We're working on [issue]. Hope to serve you better next visit."
- ★ "Sorry about this, [Name]. Please call us on [number] so we can make it right."
- Reply within 48 hours. Always.

```
Most businesses reply "Thank you!" to every Google review.
That's a missed chance.

People reading your reviews are deciding whether to choose you. Your replies show them how you treat customers.

Here are 3 simple templates:

★★★★★ Good review
"Thank you, [Name]! So glad the [service] went well. See you next time."
→ Use their name and mention the service.

★★★ Average review
"Thanks for the honest feedback, [Name]. We're working on [issue]. Hope to serve you better on your next visit."
→ Acknowledge the problem. Show you're fixing it.

★ Bad review
"We're sorry about this, [Name]. Please call us on [number] so we can make it right."
→ Stay calm. Take the conversation offline.

And one rule: reply within 48 hours, every time.

Do you reply to your Google reviews?

#GoogleReviews #LocalSEO #CustomerExperience
```

## Thu 12 Nov: carousel, the 7 parts of a service page that ranks

Poster text (8 slides):
1. Every service page that ranks has the same **7 parts**. (Swipe →)
2. 01 · A clear headline with the service + your area. "Root canal treatment in Anna Nagar", not "Our Services".
3. 02 · Who it's for. The problem in the customer's words.
4. 03 · What happens, step by step. Fear drops when people know what to expect.
5. 04 · Price, or a price range. Or what changes the price.
6. 05 · Proof. Reviews, real photos, before/after.
7. 06 · FAQs. The questions people ask on calls, answered on the page.
8. 07 · One clear next step. Call or WhatsApp, visible without scrolling. **Save this before you build your next page.**

```
Most service pages say what the business does.
The ones that rank answer what the customer wants to know.

After building and fixing many service pages, I keep seeing the same 7 parts on the ones that rank and get enquiries. 👇

1. A headline with the service and your area
2. Who it's for, in the customer's words
3. What happens, step by step
4. A price, a range, or what changes the price
5. Proof: reviews and real photos
6. FAQs from real customer calls
7. One clear next step: call or WhatsApp

Which of these is missing from your website?

## Mon 16 Nov: Google search vs ChatGPT

Poster text:
- SAME QUESTION, TWO PLACES
- "Best dental clinic near me"
- **Google and ChatGPT pick businesses differently.**
- Google: shows a list and a map · looks at relevance, distance and prominence · ads at the top
- ChatGPT: gives one direct answer · picks businesses described clearly and consistently across the web · fewer clicks, more trust in the answer
- What helps on both: accurate listings, real reviews, clear website content

```
More people now ask ChatGPT for recommendations instead of searching Google.

Same question. Two very different answers.

Google
→ Shows a list of websites and a map
→ For local results, it looks at relevance, distance and prominence
→ Ads often sit at the top

ChatGPT
→ Gives one direct answer, often with a few names
→ Tends to mention businesses that are described clearly and consistently across the web
→ People click less, but trust the answer more

The good news: the same basics help you on both.
1. Accurate Google Business Profile and listings
2. Real, recent reviews
3. A website that clearly says what you do, where, and for whom

Have you ever asked ChatGPT to recommend a local business?

#AISearch #LocalSEO #DigitalMarketing
